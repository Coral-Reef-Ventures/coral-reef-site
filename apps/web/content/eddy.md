# Before intent, there is a thought

{.lead}
Every product promise starts as something smaller: a passing idea, a decision made in a hallway, a note to ask someone about. Most never become a promise at all. Eddy is planned for that first step.

***

{.eyebrow}
The step before intent

## Most thinking is lost before anyone writes it down

{.lead}
Engineers and product managers working with agents get more done than ever, and so they have more to keep track of: ideas, decisions, questions and things to raise with someone.

Those end up in drafts, sticky notes and the last lines of a chat with an agent. Writing one down properly asks too much at the wrong moment: which folder, which project, is this a task or a note? So the thought waits, and often it is gone.

How they fit

## The loop, one step earlier

{.lead}
The home page's five steps begin at intent. We think the loop begins one step before that, when a thought is first written down.

:::steps{.lifecycle}
1. **Capture.** Get a thought down the moment you have it, without deciding where it goes. [Eddy]{.kicker .eddy}
2. **Intent.** Write down what the product promises, and who owns each promise. [Intentset]{.kicker .intentset}
3. **Execution.** Plan each change and move it forward, with people and agents on the same work. [Streamlane]{.kicker .streamlane}
4. **Verification.** See which promises have a check passing on this commit, and which have no check at all. [Intentset]{.kicker .intentset}
5. **Release.** Record what each release is meant to do for users, before it ships. [Driftline]{.kicker .driftline}
6. **Feedback.** Watch real users meet it, ask them why when adoption stalls, and take what you learn back to intent. [Driftline]{.kicker .driftline}
:::

{.note}
Capture feeds the rest of the loop. A decision becomes a promise, an action item becomes shared work, and what a team learns from use comes back as the next thing worth writing down.

What Eddy is

## A place where thinking becomes context

Eddy is planned as a workspace for engineers and product managers, on the desktop and on the phone.

- **Capture never asks a question.** A keyboard shortcut, the terminal, a phone's share sheet or an agent: every thought lands in one inbox.
- **Organizing comes after.** With your own AI key, Eddy proposes a project, people and dates for each thought, and one keystroke accepts or corrects it.
- **Agendas build themselves.** What you meant to raise with someone collects in one place for your next conversation with them.
- **Agents read the context.** Each project keeps a brief of its goals, decisions and open questions that coding agents can read and add to.

How it connects

## Built on the same foundations

Notes in Eddy are planned as Markset documents: plain text that agents write and people read, wherever the files go. A decision confirmed in Eddy can become an Intentset record, and an action item ready for the team can become a Streamlane task. Like the other four, Eddy is planned to stand on its own.
